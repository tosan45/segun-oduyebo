Who Is Segun?

A Life Devoted to Developing People

Segun Oduyebo has spent his working life on a single question: what does it take to help a person become who they were made to be? That question has taken him into pulpits and boardrooms, classrooms and community halls, start-up offices and student assemblies.

He teaches, consults, mentors, builds programmes — and writes.

My Story

A Life Devoted to Developing People

He has been a teacher, a pastor, a consultant, a mentor and a builder of programmes. Beneath every one of those roles is the same work.

Segun Oduyebo did not set out to build a career across four fields. He set out to be useful to the person in front of him, and the fields arrived on their own.

It began in a borrowed hall in 2004 with forty-two young people who had been told, in a hundred quiet ways, that not much was expected of them. Three days later they were standing up to speak. He has been chasing that moment ever since — the moment a person revises their estimate of themselves upward.

The work has never really changed. Only the rooms have.

From youth development came education work, because the young people kept describing the same broken classrooms. From education came enterprise, because the graduates could not find employers and had to become them. From enterprise came leadership consulting, because the businesses that failed rarely failed for lack of ideas. And running underneath all of it, from the beginning, has been faith — not as a separate compartment, but as the reason for the rest.

What he actually does

Four things, most weeks. He speaks — to congregations, conferences, companies, campuses. He consults — with organisations working on leadership, culture and performance. He builds and runs programmes — for young people, teachers and small business owners. And he writes.

The writing is not an afterthought. It is where the practice is examined, argued with, and made transferable. Much of what appears in the Library began as a note scribbled after a session that did not go the way he expected.

How he works

  1. Start with the person, not the programme. Structures are downstream of people.
  2. Refuse the shortcut. Development that can be completed in a weekend was probably not development.
  3. Measure the human outcome. Attendance is not impact.
  4. Leave something behind. Every engagement should outlive the engagement.
